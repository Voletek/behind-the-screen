## 1. What a Game Master Does

A GM plays several roles at once [Tier 1, Fine 1983: players move between the social frame, the
game frame and the fiction frame; the GM manages all three]:

| Role | Job | Where in this repo |
|---|---|---|
| **Narrator** | Describe the world vividly and briefly | running-the-game.md → FRAME |
| **Referee** | Apply the rules fairly and consistently | adjudication.md; systems/ |
| **World** | Play every NPC, faction and force of nature | npc-voice.md; campaign-continuity.md (fronts) |
| **Host** | Manage the social table: safety, spotlight, energy | tenets 1 and 7; session-zero.md |
| **Fan of the players** | Want the PCs to be awesome, and to struggle | Apocalypse World principle [Tier 2] |
| **Archivist** | Remember what happened | state-keeping.md |

The best GMs are not the best storytellers. They are the best *listeners*: they notice what the
players care about and put it under pressure. [Tier 2]

## 2. The Conversation

Play is a conversation: the GM says what the world presents, the players say what they do, and the
rules come in when the outcome is uncertain [Tier 2, Apocalypse World]. Everything in
`running-the-game.md` is a way to keep that conversation flowing:
- **Ask questions you don't know the answer to.** "What does your character fear about the sea?"
- **Build on answers.** A player's offhand detail is gold; make it matter later.
- **Say yes, or roll.** "No" stops the conversation. "Yes, but" and "roll for it" keep it going.

## 3. Describing the World

- **Lead with what matters**, then two or three sensory details (sound, smell, texture, temperature),
  then something to act on.
- **Use specific nouns.** "A rope bridge with three planks missing" beats "a dangerous bridge".
- **Vary the senses.** Visual descriptions dominate by default; sound and smell create presence.
- **Describe change, not inventory.** "The torches gutter as the door opens" over a list of furniture.
- **Let players fill details** when the tone allows: "What's on the wall of your old room?"
- **Text-chat specifics** [Tier 3]: short paragraphs; bold NPC names; no headings during play;
  keep mechanics in brackets so the fiction reads cleanly.

## 4. Pacing and Spotlight

See `references/PACING_AND_TENSION_REFERENCE.md`. Key ideas:
- **Scene questions** give each scene an end condition.
- **Cut aggressively.** The GM controls time; use it.
- **Tension needs release.** Players can't sustain dread for four hours.
- **Spotlight is a resource.** Distribute it on purpose, not by who types fastest.

## 5. Prep Styles

| Style | Prep is… | Good for | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Situations + secrets** (default here) | Strong start, scenes, clues, NPCs, fronts | Most games | Shea, *Lazy DM* [Tier 2] |
| **Node-based** | Locations/people (nodes) connected by clues | Mysteries, investigations | Alexander [Tier 2] |
| **Fronts and clocks** | Dangers that advance on their own | Campaigns, PbtA | Apocalypse World; Blades [Tier 2] |
| **Dungeon key** | Mapped rooms with contents | Dungeon crawls (5e, OSR) | Classic [Tier 2] |
| **Five-room dungeon** | Entrance, puzzle, setback, climax, reward | Short adventures | Johnn Four [Tier 2] |
| **Sandbox / hexcrawl** | A region with sites, factions, rumours | Player-driven exploration | OSR tradition [Tier 2] |
| **Mission** | One task, GM-set obstacles | Mouse Guard | Mouse Guard [Tier 2] |
| **Clearing** | A place under faction pressure | Root | Root [Tier 2] |

Rule across all styles: **prep situations, not plots** (Tenet 2).

## 6. Improvisation

- Improvisation is mostly **reuse**: unused prep, improv tables, and the players' own ideas.
- **The "who wants what" question** generates any scene: who is here, what do they want, what
  happens if nobody acts?
- **Name everything** and record it; an improvised innkeeper becomes canon the moment they speak.
- See `running-the-game.md` → Improvisation and `references/IMPROV_TABLES.md`.

## 7. NPCs

- **Want + voice + secret** is enough for 90% of NPCs. Full method: `npc-voice.md`.
- **Contrast** NPCs in the same scene (formal vs slangy, fast vs slow).
- **NPCs are not information dispensers.** They have reasons to share, hide or lie.
- **Villains need a plan that advances without the PCs**, and a reason the PCs take it personally.

## 8. Mysteries and Information

- **The Three Clue Rule** [Tier 2, Alexander]: for every conclusion, three clues. Players miss clues;
  redundancy is what makes mysteries fair.
- **Permissive clue-finding:** if the PC looks in the right place, they find the essential clue.
  Rolls buy extra information, speed, or cost.
- **Proactive clues:** if the players are stuck, a clue comes to them (a witness talks, the killer
  strikes again).
- **Don't gate on one roll** (adjudication.md → Clues).
- **Let players be wrong.** A wrong theory acted on is great drama; correct it with consequences, not
  with a lecture.

## 9. Combat Craft

- **Every fight has a reason** beyond "they attack": a goal, a cost, a choice.
- **Terrain and objectives** make fights memorable more than bigger numbers do.
- **Morale and exits** prevent slogs and make survival a strategy.
- **Fast GM turns:** one or two sentences per enemy, then back to the players.
- **Describe hits and misses** in the fiction, briefly ("the axe bites the shield rim").
- See `encounter-design.md` and the system profile for procedure and math.

## 10. Social and Exploration Play

- **Social:** know the NPC's want and their price; let the approach set the difficulty and the dice
  decide. Never require a shy player to act out persuasion.
- **Exploration:** give a reason to look (something hidden, a resource draining, a choice of paths).
  Wonder is a reward. Describe the impossible plainly.
- **Travel:** montage it unless something on the road matters; use the system's procedure if it has one.

## 11. Horror and Dread

- **Consent first** (Tenet 1; `references/SAFETY_TOOLS_REFERENCE.md` → Horror).
- **Implication beats explicitness.** Show the effect, withhold the cause.
- **Wrongness in the ordinary:** a familiar thing behaving slightly wrong is scarier than a monster.
- **Isolation and diminishing resources** (light, sanity, allies) drive tension.
- **Never name the unnameable** (the Keeper of the Light rule): a name makes it smaller.
- **Silence and short messages** after long ones create pauses in text play [Tier 3].

## 12. Rewards and Advancement

- Rewards tie to PC goals: information, allies, reputation, items, advancement, *closure*.
- Use the system's guidance; avoid inflation (AI failure mode #13).
- **Costly power** (Strain, debt, attention) makes rewards meaningful (see the Fractured Shadows bible).

## 13. Campaign Structures

| Structure | Shape | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| **Episodic** | Self-contained sessions, light continuity | Busy groups, one-shot regulars |
| **Serial** | Arcs with dramatic questions | Story-heavy campaigns (Fractured Shadows) |
| **Sandbox** | Player-chosen goals in a reactive world | Experienced, proactive players |
| **West Marches** | Rotating players, shared persistent world | Large communities, Discord servers |
| **Adapted canon** | A novel, album or module as the default situation | Fans of the source |

Adapted canon needs canon forks and open canon questions (`campaign-continuity.md`).

## 14. Group Dynamics

- Players want different things [Tier 2, Robin D. Laws, *Robin's Laws of Good Game Mastering*:
  player types such as the butt-kicker, tactician, specialist, method actor, storyteller, casual
  gamer]. Session zero step 2 asks; prep serves the mix.
- **Quiet players** are not uninterested; give them low-pressure spotlight (a question about their
  character, a moment that uses their skills).
- **Dominant players** get redirected, not punished: "Great. Hold that thought. Elara, what are you
  doing while this happens?"
- **Rules lawyers** are allies: make them the table's reference and keep final say (adjudication.md).
- **Problems at the table** are solved out of character, between sessions, never by punishing a PC.

## 15. Game Mastering as an AI

An AI GM is tireless, patient, always available, and able to improvise endless detail. It also has
specific weaknesses, catalogued in `references/AI_GM_FAILURE_MODES.md` [Tier 3]. The design of this
repo answers them:

| Weakness | Repo answer |
|---|---|
| Can't produce randomness | Players roll their own dice; GM rolls via `tools/roll.py` or declared simulation (Tenet 5) |
| Steers players to "the answer" | Information Discipline and the hint ladder (running-the-game.md); review A6 |
| Builds the character for the player | Player-owned creation (character-creation.md); `tools/character.py` |
| Forgets long chats | `state.json`, session logs, Save Blocks (Tenet 8) |
| Pleases the user | Failure Menu, stakes before rolls, review A3 |
| Narrates for the player | Tenet 2, review A1 |
| Writes too much | Output contract, table preferences, review A2 |
| Mixes up systems | System profiles with AI GM notes |
| Confabulates rules | Rulings labelled as rulings; rules-arbiter |
| Can't see the table | Explicit check-ins and safety tools in text |

**Human co-GM pattern:** a human player can act as co-GM, holding the rulebook, rolling the GM's
dice, and calling `OOC:` corrections. It works well for new tables. [Tier 3]

## 16. Cheat Sheets

**Before each session:** load files → validate → confirm three facts → recap → strong start.

**Every GM turn:** narration · [mechanics] · 🎲 lines · prompt to a named player.

**Should I roll?** Uncertain + interesting failure → roll. Otherwise say yes, or say the cost.

**Who rolls?** The player, for their character. Send a roll request, then wait. The GM rolls the world.

**Am I leading?** Did I suggest an action, ask a question with a plan in it, single out a clue, or
say something the characters don't know? Cut it, end on "What do you do?".

**On a failure:** harm · cost · complication · worse position · reveal a threat · tick a clock ·
separate them · hard bargain · turn their move back · plain failure (rarely).

**End of session:** wind-down → state.json → session log (6 headings) → validate → Save Block if chat-only.

**Safety:** `X` → stop, remove, move on, no questions.
